# Knowledge bank — carriers/wall materials at pH 12–14, hot-air drying, 30 °C/75% RH

Values marked **(S)** = backed by a source below. Values marked **(chưa verify)** = no source found; treat as unverified and re-measure. Do not promote an unverified value into a customer/regulatory document.

## 1. Alkaline chemistry of the feed (pH 12–14, ≤100 °C at the droplet)
- Glycosidic bonds are NOT directly hydrolysed by OH⁻ at moderate temperature. The first reaction in kraft pulping (pH 13–14) is **peeling** (β-alkoxy elimination) from the reducing end; hydrolytic cleavage of the glycosidic bond only becomes significant from **~160–170 °C** **(S: PuuKemia/TKK L5 lecture PDF)**. Consequence: in a few-seconds spray-dry residence, backbone loss is small; the real damage window is the **feed hold tank** (pH 12–14, 50–80 °C, tens of minutes).
- Peptide bonds are more alkali-stable than glycosidic bonds: complete hydrolysis needs e.g. **4.2 N NaOH, 110 °C, 18 h** **(S: Hugli & Moore 1972, J Biol Chem)**. Side reactions are faster than hydrolysis: β-elimination of cystine/phosphoserine → dehydroalanine → **lysinoalanine (LAL)** cross-links, Lys loss, racemisation **(S: Lynch 2019, PMC6764852)**.
- Breaks fast in alkali: **pectin** (β-elimination adjacent to the methyl ester; USDA ARS Krall & McFeeters; Mao 2019 PMC6690420) > **CMC** (depolymerisation at pH ≥ 11–12; Nouryon/CPKelco CMC handbook) > **inulin** (fructose/alkaline degradation, also generated by Lobry de Bruyn–Alberda van Ekenstein enolisation) ≥ starch/amylopectin with many reducing ends.
- Stable in alkali: **alginate** (industrially extracted in hot alkali; only degrades when alkaline extraction is too hot — Nogueira 2022), **cyclodextrins** (no reducing end), **HPMC/ether cellulose**, **MCC** (crystallite protection), pullulan / n-OSA starch backbone.
- Functional-group losses that matter more than backbone loss: **OSA ester saponification** (n-OSA starch loses emulsifier character), pectin demethoxylation, CMC carboxymethyl loss, casein dephosphorylation.
- **Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ present in the feed gelate alginate and pectin (egg-box) before spraying** → viscosity spike, nozzle blockage. Screen this before promising a formulation.
- Dissolved oxides/silicates/aluminosilicates at pH > 10: Al oxide/hydroxide → Al(OH)₄⁻; **kaolin and zeolite dissolve in 1–5 M NaOH** (zeolites are synthesised that way — Mameli 2025 MDPI Crystals); fumed silica → silicate. Spray-dry anti-caking silicates must be **dry-blended after drying**.

## 2. Reducing vs non-reducing (Maillard driver)
- **Reducing** (free carbonyl): maltodextrin (DE > 0; more at DE 20 than DE 10), glucose, fructose, **lactose (i.e. skim milk powder)**, maltose, starch hydrolysates.
- **Non-reducing** (both anomeric carbons engaged): sucrose (α1↔β2), trehalose (α1↔α1), inulin/fructan, cyclodextrins, pullulan (one reducing end per very long chain), MCC / CMC / HPMC, PVP (not a sugar).
- pH effect: initial step is nucleophilic addition of **free** amine to the carbonyl. At pH 12–14 α-amino (pKa ~9–10) and lysine ε-NH₂ (~10.5) are essentially fully deprotonated, so the reactive −NH₂ concentration rises steeply; OH⁻ also catalyses enolisation and Amadori rearrangement, and alkaline sugar degradation supplies a second dicarbonyl pool. The commonly quoted "few-fold per 0.5–1 pH unit" figure is **(chưa verify)** — do not quote a number.
- Consequence for an alkaline herbal extract (amino acids + reducing sugars + K⁺/Na⁺/Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺): dark melanoidins, amino-acid loss, burnt/caramel off-notes, and **organic acids generated that pull the reconstituted pH below 12**.

## 4. Moisture uptake and deliquescence
- Only **crystalline** solids have a DRH. Amorphous carriers (maltodextrin, gum arabic, inulin, CMC, pullulan) have none — they sorb progressively and cake by plasticisation.
- Sucrose crystal DRH ≈ 85% RH at 25 °C (S: RG 2013 measuring DRH); glucose ~89–91% **(chưa verify)**; trehalose crystal ~90% **(chưa verify)**, matches its lower sensitivity to high RH in the lyophilisation/moisture paper (PubMed 17004837).
- Na₂CO₃: hydration to monohydrate from ~72% RH, deliquescence at **84.5% RH** (S: Yang 2020, J Chem Eng Data) — i.e. an anhydrous carbonate alkali reserve survives 30 °C/75% RH.
- NaOH/KOH (free alkali) deliquesce at very low RH (~10% RH, secondary source — **(chưa verify)** via handbook). This is the dominant 24-month failure mode for a "pH ≥ 12" powder.
- Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (natron) melts in its own water of crystallisation at ~32–34 °C **(chưa verify** source) → unusable as a dry-blend carrier; fine as a liquid alkali source.
- Strong water uptake / cake risk: gum arabic (highest water adsorption of the tested walls — Tonon 2009; most RH-sensitive in Ramos 2022 PMC8911758), CMC-Na, inulin, sodium alginate, sodium caseinate, skim milk powder, gelatin, sodium silicate.
- Low uptake / non-deliquescent: fumed silica (E551), calcium silicate (E552), MCC, CaCO₃ (E170).

## 5. Film formation / encapsulation
- Best film-formers + surface activity: **n-OSA starches (Hi-Cap 100, Capsul, Pure-Cote)** > **gum arabic** (arabinogalactan-protein: protein anchors oil, polysaccharide builds the shell) > **WPI / sodium caseinate** (emulsification, oxygen barrier) > **pullulan** (transparent, best oxygen barrier) > **gelatin**, alginate/pectin (but Ca²⁺-gelated).
- Molecular inclusion: **β-CD / HP-β-CD** hold volatiles and mask off-notes; β-CD is only 1.85 g/100 mL soluble.
- NOT encapsulation agents, only fillers/anti-caking: maltodextrin, trehalose, sucrose, inulin, MCC, CaCO₃, silica, calcium silicate, carbonate minerals, clays/zeolites.
- Volatile retention rises with fast crust formation (surface activity), partitioning, and a **high-Tg shell** that vitrifies around the volatile.
- Caveat: n-OSA retention data are from neutral media; the OSA group is saponified at pH 12–14.

## 6. Equipment materials vs pH 12–14
| material | verdict | source |
|---|---|---|
| 316L / 316 (1.4404) | OK for dilute caustic < ~80 °C; 0.023 mm/y in aerated 50% NaOH at 25 °C | (S) BSSA selection guide; Monteiro 2019 |
| 316L above ~80 °C / 40–50% caustic | caustic **SCC** risk (safe limits ~70 °C at 50%, ~80 °C at 40%; TAPPI tests at 137 °C in 50% NaOH) | (S) Parr Instrument; TAPPI 19PEE51 |
| 304 | not recommended (chloride pitting + SCC; the herbal feed has Cl⁻) | (S) BSSA |
| PTFE, PP, HDPE | inert to alkali at all concentrations within their temperature limits (PTFE all; PP ~90–100 °C; HDPE ~70–90 °C) | general chemical-resistance data — cite as such |
| soda-lime glass | attacked at pH > 9–10, congruent silicate dissolution; surface flaking seen within 24 h at pH 9 | (S) ScienceDirect glass corrosion; Lee 2023 PMC10245155; Zanini 2023 |
| aluminium | oxide stable only pH ~4–9, dissolves as Al(OH)₄⁻ above | (S) Alumeco knowledge base |
| alumina ceramic nozzles | also attacked by hot alkali → prefer tungsten carbide, or 316L + PTFE lining | practical inference from Al oxide solubility |

## 7. Vietnam / international additive status (as used in the ranking)
With INS and generally permitted: dextrin/maltodextrin **1400**, gum arabic **414**, n-OSA starch **1450** (Codex GSFA), sodium alginate **401**, pectin **440**, CMC **466**, HPMC **464**, silicon dioxide **551**, calcium silicate **552**, MCC **460(i)**, calcium carbonate **170**, β-cyclodextrin **459**, pullulan **1204** (EU, QS in 2 categories; EFSA PMC11897799), PVP **1201** (EU permits only in table-top sweetener tablets → not for a powder supplement).
Food ingredients rather than additives (no INS): trehalose, sucrose, inulin (Coussement 1999), WPI, sodium caseinate, skim milk powder, gelatin.
Not permitted as food additives: **sodium silicate (INS 550)**, **HP-β-CD** (pharma excipient only — EMA 2017), bentonite, zeolite (clinoptilolite), kaolin (Codex 559 is aluminium silicate, not kaolin).
Intermediate: trona/natron have no INS of their own; their constituents Na₂CO₃ **500(i)** and NaHCO₃ **500(ii)** are permitted → declare by composition and check the group limit.
**Always** cross-check the actual row/limit in Phụ lục I of **TT 24/2019/TT-BYT** before any registration or label claim — the statuses above were derived from Codex/EU plus the VN framework, not from a full-text read of the annex **(chưa verify** for clause-level citations).
