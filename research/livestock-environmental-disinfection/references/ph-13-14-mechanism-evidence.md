# pH 13-14 Disinfection — Mechanism & Evidence Bank

Compiled October 2026 from 291 PubMed publications + WOAH disease cards. Source document: `ban-do-benh-lon-vietnam.html` (previously published).

## The 4 killing mechanisms of high pH

1. **Saponification (OH⁻ cuts lipid esters):** OH⁻ ions cleave ester bonds in phospholipids — the same reaction as soap-making. Enveloped viruses lose membrane fusion ability immediately. Gram-negative bacteria lose outer membrane then lyse. 17/17 enveloped viruses and 13/13 Gram-negative bacteria in the catalog die at pH ≤ 11.5.
   - Evidence: NaOH 0.1 M (pH ≈ 13) inactivates pseudorabies, parvovirus, pestivirus in 30 seconds (PMID 10208725, but at 60°C). Influenza A H1N1 completely killed in solution; surface test 1 min → 3.7 log₁₀ reduction (PMID 20430477).

2. **Protein denaturation:** At pH > 11, –SH, –OH of tyrosine and amide of asparagine/glutamine become ionized/deamidated; tertiary structure collapses. Direct visual evidence: at pH 11.2, rotavirus VP4 spikes detach from capsid (PMID 1649333) — virus intact but cannot attach to cells.

3. **RNA cleavage (alkaline hydrolysis):** RNA has 2′-OH on ribose; at high pH this group deprotonates then attacks the adjacent phosphodiester bond, self-cleaving the RNA strand. DNA lacks 2′-OH so it's far more alkali-stable. Consequence: NaOH 0.25 N in 1 hour degrades RNA virus genome below detection (PMID 27260412). ssDNA viruses (circovirus, parvovirus) are the most alkali-resistant group.

4. **Proton gradient collapse in bacteria:** Bacteria rely on H⁺ gradient across membrane to drive ATP synthase. When external pH reaches 12-13, the gradient reverses, proton pump cannot compensate, intracellular pH cannot be maintained → cell death even before membrane rupture. Measured directly: pH 10.0-11.0 for 30 min completely kills Salmonella with full membrane integrity loss (PMID 12513986).

## Role of Ca²⁺ and K⁺ (specific to this formulation)

- **Ca(OH)₂ alone cannot reach pH 13-14.** Solubility of Ca(OH)₂ is ~1.7 g/L at 20°C → saturated lime water stops at pH 12.4-12.6. To reach pH 13-14, a fully soluble strong alkali is required (KOH in this formulation).
- **Ca(OH)₂ acts as an "alkali reservoir."** Undissolved Ca(OH)₂ particles remain as solid; when OH⁻ in solution is consumed by organic matter or CO₂, more Ca(OH)₂ dissolves to compensate. This is the buffer mechanism that maintains pH on surfaces longer.
- **Downside:** Ca(OH)₂ reacts with atmospheric CO₂ → CaCO₃ (carbonation). White lime residue visible ≠ active. Ca²⁺ also precipitates with phosphate and protein, consuming OH⁻ in dirty environments.
- **K⁺ role:** Contributes osmotic stress and ion transport competition, but the killing agent is OH⁻. Practical advantage of KOH over NaOH: less concrete corrosion at equal pH.

## Critical caveats

1. **The 30-second claim is misleading.** Many studies reporting "30 seconds kill" were performed at 60°C, not barn temperature. Same study: NaOH 1.0 M (pH ≈ 14) at 15°C did NOT rapidly inactivate hepatitis A virus (a non-enveloped picornavirus) (PMID 10208725).
2. **PEDV is not killed at pH 9.2 at 4°C.** The commonly cited "pH 9.2 reduces 8 log₁₀ in 30 min" applies only at ≥40°C. At barn temperature (15-32°C), efficacy is much lower (PMID 26602207).
3. **Inactivation = f(pH × time × temperature).** Same pH 13-14, disinfection at noon (32°C) is much faster than early morning (15°C). Dilute with warm water and extend wet contact time when it's cold.
4. **Organic matter neutralizes alkali.** ASFV survives 21 hours at pH 13.4 WITHOUT serum, but up to 7 DAYS WITH serum (WOAH disease card). pH 14 sprayed on a floor with blood does NOT guarantee ASFV kill. **Wash before disinfecting — this is not optional.**
5. **pH 13-14 is for EMPTY barns only.** With animals present, safe pH drops to ≤9.5, which only kills 9/60 pathogens. For occupied barns, use acid + essential oil formulations (pH 3-4, safe for pigs).

## Why not just use lime water (pH 12.4)?

- Saturated Ca(OH)₂ pH 12.4-12.6 is below the threshold for PCV2/parvovirus (pH ≥ 12.8 needed, PMID 12935806)
- No KOH → no pH buffer → pH drops faster on contact with organic matter
- The Ca(OH)₂ "reservoir" effect is weaker at pH 12.4 than at pH 13-14 with KOH
