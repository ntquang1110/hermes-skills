# Psychology of Leadership — Module 3 Concepts

7 psychology concepts for leadership coaching, designed as re-engagement hooks when users stall on earlier action tasks.

## 1. Social Learning Theory (Bandura) — Leadership by Example

**Core:** People learn by observing others (modeling), not just direct experience. The leader is the #1 role model — team copies behavior, not words.

**Bobo Doll Experiment (Bandura, 1961):** Children watched adults hit a doll → 88% imitated the aggression in the same room vs 0% in control group.

**Application for stuck users:**
- *"Nếu team bắt chước y hệt hành vi của anh hôm nay, anh có hài lòng không?"*
- Connects to: the "doing instead of leading" trap. If leader always jumps in to solve, team learns to wait for the answer.

## 2. System 1 & System 2 (Kahneman) — Why Leaders Make Bad Decisions

**Core:** Brain has 2 systems. System 1 = fast, automatic, emotional. System 2 = slow, logical, energy-intensive. 95% of daily decisions by System 1.

**Mars Climate Orbiter ($327M loss):** Metric vs imperial unit error. Root cause: confirmation bias — everyone assumed it was right.

**Pre-Mortem technique:** Before deciding, ask "What would make this assumption WRONG?" then find disconfirming evidence.

**Application:** For users who react quickly → "pause 6 seconds" gets scientific backing. It's activating System 2.

## 3. Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan) — Real Motivation

**Core:** 3 innate psychological needs — Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness. When all 3 are met → intrinsic motivation explodes. When blocked → burnout.

**Overjustification Effect:** Rewards DECREASE intrinsic motivation. Kids who drew for fun → got rewarded → stopped drawing when rewards stopped.

**Google 20% time:** Gmail, AdSense, Google News born from autonomy.

**Application for leadership coaching:**
- Team not proactive? Check which of 3 is missing.
- User wants team to brainstorm → they need Autonomy (choose how), Competence (training), Relatedness (belonging).
- **Best hook for stalled Feedback Friday:** Feedback is the gateway to Relatedness. Without knowing how team sees you, they don't feel psychologically safe.

## 4. Stanford Prison Experiment & Lucifer Effect (Zimbardo) — Power Corrupts

**Core:** Ordinary people, given absolute power without checks, become abusive. Not "bad people" — "bad system" turns normal people.

**SPE (1971):** 24 normal students, random guard/prisoner assignment. Day 6: guards torturing prisoners, 1/3 prisoners broken. Stopped early.

**Modern research (Liao, 2021):** Power reduces prefrontal cortex activity (impulse control). Explains why powerful leaders lose control.

**Application:**
- Self-check: When was the last time someone told you "you're wrong"? If >3 months → problem.
- Do you create safe space for dissent?
- **Hook for stalled EQ Journal:** Power makes you less self-aware. Journaling is the antidote.

## 5. Evolutionary Psychology of Followership & Prestige

**Core:** Humans evolved to follow leaders who signal competence (dominance) AND generosity (prestige). People follow prestigious leaders voluntarily; dominant leaders get compliance, not commitment.

**Research:** Henrich & Gil-White (2001) — prestige = freely conferred status based on expertise + willingness to share. Dominance = status through fear.

**Application:**
- User managing older team → dominance doesn't work (they've seen 20 bosses). Prestige does: show expertise AND share credit.
- *"Why would anyone FOLLOW you, not just work for you?"*

## 6. Dark Triad in Leadership (Machiavellianism, Narcissism, Psychopathy)

**Core:** 3 personality traits linked to toxic leadership. Moderate narcissism helps in interviews/crisis. High levels destroy culture long-term.

**Research:** Babiak & Hare (2006) — 3.9% of corporate executives meet psychopathy criteria (vs 1% general population). Dark Triad leaders rise faster but crash harder.

**Application:**
- Help user check their own blind spots: "Khi anh quyết định nhanh, anh có bỏ qua ý kiến người khác không? Đó có thể là điểm mạnh, cũng có thể là narcissistic blind spot."
- Not about diagnosis — about self-awareness.

## 7. Cognitive Dissonance (Festinger) — Why People Resist Change

**Core:** When beliefs and actions conflict, the mind feels tension → changes EITHER the belief or the action. People usually change the belief (rationalize) rather than change behavior.

**$1 vs $20 experiment (Festinger, 1957):** Subjects did boring task, paid $1 or $20 to lie about it. $1 group later said task was "enjoyable" (changed belief to justify lie). $20 group said it was boring (had external justification).

**Application for stalled users:**
- User says "I want to be a better leader" but doesn't do action tasks. Dissonance: "I'm a good leader" vs "I'm not doing the work." Resolution: rationalize "these tasks aren't important."
- **As a coach:** Gently surface the dissonance. *"Anh nói muốn lead better, nhưng 5 ngày rồi chưa làm task nào. Có chuyện gì?"* — the discomfort itself motivates change.
